Press Train. Watch the true RMSE fall even though the loss being minimised never mentions the true solution. The network is not being shown the answer; it is being told what the answer must satisfy.
Worth doing. Drag collocation points down to about 10 and retrain. The loss still reaches a small value, because the equation really is satisfied at those ten places, and the curve between them wanders. That is the failure mode to understand: a PINN's loss tells you about the points you chose, and nothing whatsoever about the points you did not. Then untick impose it exactly and watch a whole new problem appear, which tab 1 of the booklet's Chapter 10 discusses: the trivial solution u = 0 satisfies the equation perfectly, so only the initial condition stands between you and nonsense.

Set the assumed damping well away from 0.20 and train. The error against the truth climbs, and the two numbers you would actually be monitoring stay small. This is the failure mode that matters most, because nothing on your dashboard goes red.
A physical prior is only worth the extent to which it is true, exactly as Week 4's architectural priors were. The difference is that a wrong physical prior is more dangerous than a wrong architectural one, because it comes with the authority of an equation and a loss that looks healthy.
