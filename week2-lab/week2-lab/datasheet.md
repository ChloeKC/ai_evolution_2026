# Datasheet: `data/lending_data.csv` (Week 2)

*After Gebru et al., "Datasheets for Datasets" (2021). Short version.*

## Motivation
Created for the Week 2 lab of *The Evolution of Machine Learning and AI* (SETU) to show
how bias enters a pipeline. It is **synthetic**: no real person is in it. It was built so
that both groups have identical underlying ability to repay, and every measurable
disparity is manufactured by a known mechanism that can be switched off.

## Composition
- 8,000 rows, one per applicant. `applicant_id` 1–8000.
- Permitted features (7): `income`, `savings`, `employment_years`, `existing_debt`,
  `credit_history_months`, `postcode_score`, `age`.
- Protected attribute: `group` (A: 6,372 rows, 79.6 %; B: 1,628 rows, 20.4 %).
- Target: `repaid` (the **recorded** outcome; 1 = repaid). Rate: A 0.547, B 0.437.
- Diagnostic columns that would **not exist in real life** and must never be used as
  features or targets: `repaid_true` (what actually happened) and `latent` (true ability).
- No missing values. No duplicates.

## Collection process
Generated by `datasets.make_lending_data(n=8000, seed=1)` with all four bias mechanisms on
(`data/lending_data.json` records the settings). Regenerating with the same seed gives the
identical file.

## Known biases (deliberate)
| mechanism | pipeline stage | effect |
|---|---|---|
| `sampling_bias` | Data | group B is 20 % of rows, not 50 % |
| `historical_bias` | World | at equal ability, B has lower recorded income and savings and shorter credit history |
| `proxy` | Data | `postcode_score` almost perfectly reveals group (recoverable at 99.6 %) |
| `label_bias` | Labels | some B borrowers who repaid are recorded as defaulted |

## Recommended uses
Teaching and auditing fairness metrics and mitigations. Any conclusion drawn from it
about real lending is invalid: the numbers are illustrative.

## Not recommended
Training any model intended for real decisions; benchmarking real fairness interventions.
