How long the memory lasts

The point. In a plain RNN the rate at which the past fades is fixed by the weights, the same for every input and every dimension, and set during training for the average case. In an LSTM the forget gate is computed fresh at every step from the current input, so the network can decide this is worth keeping and this is noise on a case-by-case basis. Gates are not extra capacity. They are learned control over how fast the past is forgotten, and that is why an LSTM has three of them.

The same network, unrolled

Every copy of W below is the same matrix, not a different one. That is weight sharing across time, and it is why the parameter count does not depend on the sequence length.

Parameters, counted

The comparison worth remembering

A CNN shares one small kernel across every position in space. An RNN shares one weight matrix across every position in time. They are the same idea applied to different axes, and both buy the same two things: a parameter count that does not grow with the input size, and the ability to handle inputs longer or larger than anything seen in training. The difference is what they pay. A CNN's positions are independent, so they compute in parallel. An RNN's steps depend on each other, so they cannot, and that single fact is most of why transformers displaced recurrence once the hardware became wide rather than fast.
