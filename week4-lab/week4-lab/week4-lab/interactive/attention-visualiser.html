The four stages, for that one query

Raw dot products, then divided by the square root of the width, then softmax, then used as mixing weights over the values.

The arithmetic, written out

Why divide by the square root of the width? A dot product of two vectors with d entries grows roughly like the square root of d. Without the division, a wider model produces larger scores, the softmax saturates on whichever score happens to be biggest, and the gradient through it collapses towards zero. It is a scaling fix for a numerical problem, and it is the same family of problem as Week 3's vanishing gradient. Set sharpness very high to see saturation happen: one weight goes to 1, the rest to 0, and nothing can be learned from a flat gradient.

This is the single most important property on the page, and it is a mathematical fact rather than a quirk of training. With no positional information, attention computes a function of the set of tokens. It cannot distinguish dog bites man from man bites dog, because nothing in softmax(Q KT) refers to where anything is. Tick the box and the difference becomes large, because now each token's vector carries a fingerprint of its position. Every transformer ever shipped does something equivalent, and this number is why.

Four heads, one layer, one sentence

Each head has its own query and key projections, so each learns its own notion of what counts as relevant. They run in parallel and their outputs are concatenated.

A single head produces one set of weights per token, so it has to express everything it cares about in a single weighted average. A verb needs its subject, its object and its clause boundary, and blending those into one average loses all three. Multi-head attention splits the width into independent slices and lets each slice specialise, at no extra cost in parameters.
Worth doing. Compare the agreement head with the position head. One is entirely about content and ignores distance completely; the other is entirely about distance and ignores content completely. Both are the same operation with different projection matrices, which is the point: attention does not have a built-in notion of what matters. It has a built-in notion of looking things up by similarity, and what counts as similar is learned.

The crossover

Attention buys a path length of 1 at any distance, which is what makes long-range relations learnable at all. It pays in operations that grow with the square of the sequence length. That is the transformer's bargain, and it is why context windows were small for years and why a great deal of current research is about making this table less ugly.
